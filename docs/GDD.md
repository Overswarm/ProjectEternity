# PROJECT ETERNITY — Game Design Document

*A real-time civilization garden. You are not the mayor of every town. You are the current
that bends the river.*

**Version:** MVP 0.1 · **Engine:** Unity (built-in render pipeline, code-only, primitive shapes)
**Status:** Shape defined by the creative director; details filled in by the design/engineering pass (this document). Balance pass comes later — every tunable number lives in one file (`Tuning.cs`).

---

## 1. Vision

### 1.1 The One-Sentence Pitch
Watch a living civilization grow across eons in real time; you don't command it — you *influence* it, and it answers back.

### 1.2 The Core Fantasy
You are the eternal spirit of a people — part founder, part government, part myth. In the beginning your word is law: you place the first city, you name the first faith. But civilizations are made of people, and people have their own momentum. As your nation grows from one mud-brick village to a continental power, your control *decays by design*. Cities develop personalities shaped by the land under them, the neighbors beside them, and the laws you gave their grandparents. Your job shifts from *doing* to *steering*: promote this, suppress that, answer the crisis on your desk, and live with what your people do with it.

### 1.3 Design Pillars
1. **The world simulates, you influence.** Nothing waits for a "next turn" button. Cities grow, drift, trade, pray, riot, and raid on their own. Every player verb is an act of *pressure*, not puppetry — with two exceptions (founding the first city and answering Decisions), everything else is a push, not a command.
2. **Geography is destiny (until technology isn't).** A desert city under harsh law becomes hungry and warlike. A forest-river city becomes carpenters and fishermen — until the forest is gone and the river becomes the engine of industry. The map writes the first draft of every city's story; technology and policy write the revisions.
3. **Happiness is behavior, not a number.** There is a happiness value under the hood, but the player experiences it as *what people do*: emigration waves, riots that burn districts, cities that secede, golden ages that erupt into festivals and genius.
4. **Time is the epic.** A session spans ~6,000 years. Eras pass; the simulation's own speed changes texture (ancient centuries flash by, modern decades crawl). The fun is watching consequences ripen over eons — the law you passed in the Bronze Age is the revolt you get in the Industrial one.
5. **Legible emergence.** Every autonomous act the simulation takes is announced in plain language in the chronicle ("The warlords of Ashfall, hungry and proud, ride against Riverbend"). If the player asks "why did that happen," the UI can answer.

### 1.4 What This Game Is Not
- Not a unit-micromanagement RTS. There is no "select 40 tanks."
- Not a turn-based optimizer. There are no per-turn yields to min-max.
- Not a pure idle game. Neglect is punished; attention and judgment are the skill.

## 2. The World

### 2.1 Map
- Square grid of tiles (default **64×64**, one large continent with ragged coasts, generated from seeded noise + radial falloff). Rendered in 3D: each tile is a colored box whose height = elevation. Rivers are bright strips laid over tiles.
- **Biomes:** Ocean, Coast, Plains, Grassland, Forest, Desert, Hills, Mountain, Tundra, Snow. Each has a color, movement cost, and base yields.
- **Rivers:** traced from mountain sources to the sea. River tiles gain food and commerce — and in the Industrial era they gain *industry* (water power → factories), deliberately echoing the pillar: the river that fed fishermen becomes the river that turns turbines.
- **Tile yields (base units):** Food (feeds population), Materials (wood/stone/ore → city development), Commerce (gold), plus intangibles generated by people (Knowledge, Faith).
- **The land changes.** Forest tiles hold a finite timber stock. Cities with heavy workshop districts consume nearby forests; a depleted forest permanently becomes grassland (chronicled: *"The old woods around Duskmere have fallen silent."*). This shifts the city's own character over time — the carpenter town industrializes.

### 2.2 Time
- Real-time with pause and speed steps (⏸, 1×, 2×, 4×, 8×).
- The base flow of **years-per-real-second decreases as eras advance** (Neolithic ≈ 9 yr/s down to Information ≈ 1.2 yr/s at 1×). Early history montages past; late history is dense with decisions. Game starts at 4000 BC; the *Long Count* ends at 2500 AD (see Legacy victory).
- The simulation ticks in fixed sub-steps (≤ 0.25 years) so behavior is stable at any speed.

## 3. Cities — the Protagonists

Cities are the real characters of the game. The player never "builds a granary." Cities build themselves; the player shapes *who they are*.

### 3.1 City Personality (the soul)
Every city carries a six-axis personality vector, each axis 0..1:

| Axis | Low | High |
|---|---|---|
| **Aggression** | peaceable | raiding, militarist |
| **Piety** | secular | devout |
| **Openness** | insular | cosmopolitan |
| **Industry** | pastoral | crafts & manufacture |
| **Commerce** | subsistence | mercantile |
| **Scholarship** | traditional | inquisitive |

Personality **drifts continuously** toward three attractors:
1. **Geography** (computed at founding from surrounding tiles, and recomputed when the land changes): deserts breed aggression and piety; mountains breed industry; rivers/coasts breed commerce and openness; forests breed industry(craft) and self-reliance; rich farmland breeds contentment (low aggression).
2. **National policy** (the player's law): authoritarian law suppresses openness; theocracy raises piety; militarism raises aggression; free markets raise commerce; science spending raises scholarship.
3. **Neighbors & faith:** personalities bleed between nearby cities; a dominant fervent religion raises piety and unity but drags scholarship.

### 3.2 Districts (emergent specialization)
Cities autonomously invest their production into six districts: **Farms, Workshops, Markets, Academies, Temples, Walls.** Investment weights come from personality + national spending priorities + immediate pressures (hunger forces farms; threats force walls). District levels multiply the matching yields.

From dominant districts + personality, each city earns a readable **specialization title** shown on the map — *Forge City, Holy City, Free Port, Academy City, Garrison Town, Breadbasket* — which also grants a small themed bonus. Nobody assigns these; they *happen*, and they match the land ("a town near the mountains is heavy into metallurgy" falls out of ore-rich tiles → workshop investment → Forge City).

### 3.3 Population & Happiness → Behavior
- Population grows from food surplus, health tech, and mood; it is capped by development (districts) and racked by famine, plague, and war.
- **The ancient ceiling:** every city's growth curve is logistic against a health asymptote (`PlateauPop × Health²`). Pre-modern cities are demographic sinks — they plateau at a few thousand souls and suffer recurring plagues when pressed against the ceiling. Medicine is what finally ends the cycle and unlocks the modern population explosion. This one curve is what makes eras take eras.
- **Happiness** is computed from: food security, faith unity, *alignment between the city's personality and your national policy* (a free-spirited port under authoritarian law is miserable; a devout desert city under theocracy is serene), war weariness, recent raids, crowding, welfare spending, golden ages.
- Happiness is expressed **only through behavior**:
  - **> 70:** growth bonus; contributes to national Golden Ages.
  - **< 45:** emigration — people literally walk to happier cities, *including foreign ones* (population defection is the quiet war).
  - **< 40:** unrest accumulates → **riots** (a district level burns down, chronicled).
  - **sustained unrest:** **secession** — the city declares itself the *Free People of X*, a new gray rebel civ at war with you. Other unhappy cities may join it.
- **Autonomous raiding (signature mechanic):** an aggressive city with scarce resources and a weaker neighbor in reach will, *on its own*, launch raids to steal food and gold — even at peace, even if you didn't ask — unless your military stance is Defensive or you spend Influence to restrain that city. Raids sour relations and start wars. Your hawks can drag you into conflicts; that's the point.

## 4. The Player — Leading Without Commanding

### 4.1 Direct Powers (rare, absolute)
- Found the **first city** (choose the starting tile — the single most consequential click of the game).
- Answer **Decisions** (see 4.4).
- End-of-flow actions: declare war / sue for peace / send gifts; adopt or suppress a religion; raise an army from a city's garrison and aim it (Raid or Conquer); train a settler.

### 4.2 Influence (political capital)
A regenerating resource (regen scales with your people's average happiness — legitimacy). Spent on everything that pushes society around:

| Act | Cost (approx) |
|---|---|
| Shift a policy axis | 15–40 (scales with size of shift) |
| Adopt a state religion | 30 |
| Suppress a religion | 40 (and its faithful hate you) |
| Restrain a raiding city | 10 |
| Set research focus | 10 |
| Diplomatic acts | 10–25 |

If your people are miserable, your Influence dries up precisely when you need it — unpopular governments can't act.

### 4.3 Policy (the national character)
Six sliders, each a slow-moving societal value. The player sets a *target*; society drifts toward it over years (inertia), and cities whose personality clashes with the law lose happiness.

- **Authority** (liberty ↔ authoritarian) — order & control vs. openness & science
- **Devotion** (secular ↔ theocratic) — unity & stability vs. discovery; theocracies are calm but prone to abrupt religious swings
- **Militarism** (pacifist ↔ warlike)
- **Expansion** (consolidate ↔ manifest destiny) — drives autonomous settler waves
- **Openness** (closed ↔ open borders/trade) — commerce, science, faith spread — in *both* directions
- **Market** (collectivist ↔ free market) — gold vs. equality (welfare feel)

Plus **national spending allocation** (Military / Infrastructure / Science / Welfare) and a **military stance** (Defensive / Balanced / Aggressive) that gates autonomous raiding.

### 4.4 Decisions (the crises on your desk)
Contextual dilemmas generated by the actual simulation state — plague outbreaks, famines, new faiths, first contacts, revolt threats, the conscription question when iron is mastered, the smog question when factories rise. Each offers 2–3 options with real mechanical + cultural consequences, an expiry (indecision auto-resolves to the default and costs you legitimacy), and optional auto-pause. AI leaders answer the same dilemmas according to their own personalities.

Examples shipped in MVP: Plague Response, Famine Relief, A New Faith, First Contact, The Iron Question (army model), Revolt Ultimatum, Golden Age celebration, The Smoke Question (industrial pollution vs. output), War Rally, Succession of the Free City.

## 5. National Systems

### 5.1 Technology
- A single **Knowledge** pool per civ, fed by city scholarship. Research is **automatic** — society discovers on its own — but the player may pay Influence to set a **focus** (Military / Economy / Science / Faith / Civic) that weights what gets discovered.
- **45 discoveries across 9 eras** (Neolithic → Bronze → Iron → Classical → Medieval → Renaissance → Industrial → Modern → Information), each a compounding multiplier (food, industry, commerce, science, health, military, housing). Era = f(discoveries known). The 46th "tech" is the **Ascension Project** (see Victory).
- Open, secular, scholarly societies research dramatically faster; that is their compensation for being harder to keep unified.

### 5.2 Religion
- Faiths **found themselves** in high-piety cities (a prophet event), each with a name, a color, and a **fervor** value (fervent = strong unity, aggressive spread, science drag; contemplative = mild everything).
- Faiths spread city-to-city by proximity and openness; every city holds *shares* of multiple faiths (plus folk belief).
- Player levers: **Adopt** as state religion (unity in believer cities, resentment in others), **Suppress** (its faithful seethe — good luck), or **Tolerate**. Freedom of religion (low Devotion + high Openness) yields more discoveries and more religious friction; theocracy yields serenity, slower science, and the occasional zealous convulsion — exactly the trade described in the vision.

### 5.3 Diplomacy & War (deliberately light for MVP)
- Pairwise relations drift from: shared/clashing faiths, border pressure, openness, gifts, and — above all — raids.
- Wars are declared (by you, by AIs, or *for* you by your own raiders). Armies are abstract strength-stacks that march overland, visualized as moving blocks. Raids steal and burn; conquest transfers the city (and its resentful population) to the victor. War weariness gnaws at everyone's happiness until peace.
- Conquered and unhappy populations remember. Integration is a happiness problem, not a checkbox.

### 5.4 Rival Civilizations
3 AI civs (default) with leader personalities (aggression / expansion / scholarship / zeal) that set their own policies, answer their own decisions, expand, scheme, and fight. Rebel civs born from secessions join the board as gray "Free Peoples."

## 6. Victory & Defeat — *four ways to be remembered*

| Victory | Condition | Playstyle |
|---|---|---|
| **Dominion** | Control ≥ 60% of the world's population | The conqueror |
| **Ascension** | Complete the Ascension Project (post-Information mega-research) | The scientist |
| **Harmony** | ≥ 5 cities, national happiness ≥ 78 held for 120 consecutive years with *no* city below 60, at peace, Medieval era+ | The gardener |
| **Legacy** | Highest Legacy score when the Long Count ends (2500 AD) | The long game — everyone is always in this race |

Legacy accrues continuously from population, discoveries, golden-age years, and cities standing. **Defeat:** lose your last city. The game announces victory progress so rivals' trajectories create urgency.

## 7. Presentation (MVP)

- **Look:** flat-shaded colored boxes. Terrain = height-scaled boxes; cities = civ-colored cylinders that grow taller/wider with population, name + population + specialization floated above in text; armies = marching cubes; settlers = white spheres; rivers = blue strips. Selection ring, owned-territory tinting.
- **Camera:** free RTS camera — WASD/arrows pan, wheel zoom, Q/E rotate, click to select.
- **UI:** immediate-mode (code-only) — top bar (year/era, resources, influence, speed), left tabbed panel (Empire / Faith / Diplomacy / Victory), right panel (selected city), bottom chronicle (the event log narrating everything the simulation does), centered Decision modals, tile tooltip.
- **The Chronicle** is a first-class feature: history should read like history.

## 8. Session Shape (intended experience)
**Minutes 0–2:** pick the starting tile (coast? river? mountains?) — this decides your people's temperament. **Minutes 2–10:** watch the first cities sprout and drift into personalities; set founding policies; meet neighbors; first faith appears. **Mid game:** the map fills; raids spark wars you didn't order; decisions arrive faster than influence regenerates; you triage. **Late game:** industry eats the forests, cities swell, victory races become explicit; either you ascend/dominate/harmonize, or the Long Count ends and history judges the totals.

## 9. Balance & Extension Hooks (for the fine-tune pass)
- **Every constant lives in `Tuning.cs`** — growth rates, drift rates, era speeds, costs, thresholds — annotated and grouped.
- Tech catalog, decision catalog, and biome tables are declarative lists — trivially editable.
- Post-MVP candidates (explicitly out of scope now): trade routes as visible caravans; wonders; sea travel & naval raids; espionage; per-city leaders/great people; save/load; multiplayer; real art.

## 10. Technical Notes
- Pure-code project: `Bootstrap` builds the entire scene at Play (camera, light, world, UI). No prefabs, no scene wiring, no external assets — safe to regenerate art later.
- Deterministic-ish: one seeded RNG drives worldgen + sim.
- Sim layer (`Assets/Scripts/Sim`) has no dependency on views; views poll.
- **Headless balance harness** (`tools/balance-sim/`): runs entire 6,500-year worlds in seconds against a UnityEngine stub — era pacing, war frequency, victory distribution, NaN checks. The initial tuning in `Tuning.cs` was fitted with it; use it for the fine-tune pass (`dotnet run` inside the folder).
